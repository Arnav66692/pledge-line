## What still holds the score back

1. **Independent benefit evidence.** No nonprofit, donor, sponsor or independent supplier has been observed using the product. Retention, staff time saved, willingness to pay and sponsor economics remain unmeasured. More implemented features cannot supply those observations. The user deferred usability work until the software was finished; no outreach or fabricated results replaced it.
2. **Demonstrated AI advantage.** The guarded negotiation tied the strong baseline, while the unguarded first version overpaid. Final goal drafting also tied its explicit-field baseline and took more time and API credits. These results support useful safeguards and a working draft interface, not superior AI pricing or a proven language advantage.
3. **Production and operating dependencies.** A shared demo PIN, one controlled merchant and project-owned suppliers establish a sandbox demonstration. A legal merchant, donor ownership checks, sponsor authority, independent supplier permissions and applicable PayPal onboarding are still required. Free hosting has no demonstrated SLA; the initial HTTP503 and asset timeout remain unexplained. See [live-readiness requirements](LIVE-READINESS-2026-11-05.md).

The technology and presentation scores were consistently strong. The lowest scores concerned impact and feasibility. No unchanged implementation was rerolled to obtain a favorable number, and no unsupported score uplift is claimed. A later assessment should follow material new evidence rather than another attempt at the same score.
