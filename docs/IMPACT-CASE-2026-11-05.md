# Impact case and validation boundary

Pledge Line targets small nonprofits whose donors need to change or recover a recurring gift outside staffed hours. Eligible sponsor-backed hardship relief is the lead use case. Purchasing is an additional task for that same nonprofit, not a separate consumer marketplace pitch.

## Evidence that the problem matters

M+R's 2026 Benchmarks Study includes data from 180 participating nonprofits. Its fundraising chapter reports that monthly giving represented 27% of online revenue in 2025, and 22% for its small-organization cohort. That cohort has annual online revenue below $1 million. These are study-participant statistics, not a claim about every nonprofit or this app's customers. [Study and methodology](https://mrbenchmarks.com/), [fundraising findings](https://mrbenchmarks.com/fundraising/), checked November 5, 2026.

**Inference:** recurring gifts are material enough for this cohort that a reliable donor-service experience is a plausible operational priority. The study does not prove that donors want this product, that after-hours service is their main difficulty, or that sponsor-funded refunds improve retention.

## What this implementation establishes

- An eligible fictional donor received a confirmed $25 sandbox refund after a $25 sponsor capture. The recurring gift remained active. Equal gross amounts do not establish fee-free nonprofit coverage.
- The buyer-approved controlled supplier purchase completed with authoritative payment evidence and recovered across process replacement.
- Anonymous reviewers can now explore the accepted evidence and verify an original receipt or detect an edited copy without private credentials or another payment.
- A frozen comparison exposed model overpayment relative to deterministic negotiation. The original negative result remains published. The resulting price guard is evaluated separately and must not be described as proof that AI beats the supplier-floor baseline.

These are implementation and laboratory results. No observed target-user benefit, retention increase, staff time saving, independent supplier fulfillment or production revenue is claimed.

## What an actual usability and outcome study must measure

When willing participants are available, use the same three tasks for each participant: check a gift, request an eligible change with explicit consent, and identify the completed result and its evidence. Record completion, errors, requests for help and elapsed time. Keep typed and microphone results separate. Obtain permission before collecting any personal or donation information.

For nonprofit operators, measure whether a task is resolved without staff intervention, whether the financial result is understood, and the operational cost per resolved eligible task. Compare against the existing service process. Evaluate sponsor cost and fees separately from donor retention. A retained ACTIVE status in a test is not a retention uplift.

The user asked to finish the software before participating. No self-reported study results or invented testimonials have been substituted for that missing evidence.
